About RAO

An organization built around adoptees — not around adoption.

Our work begins with the people who lived the experience. RAO exists to connect Russian and former-Soviet adoptees, make difficult information easier to navigate, preserve heritage, and build a durable collective voice.

Why we exist

Russian and former-Soviet adoptees often reach adulthood with questions that cross identity, culture, law, government records, family history, citizenship, language, and belonging. Those questions can surface years or decades after adoption.

RAO is being built as a lasting home for that complexity: a place where adoptees can find each other, share lived experience, locate practical resources, preserve cultural connection, and advocate on issues that affect our community.

Our operating principles

01 · Advocacy

Adoptee rights matter

Our policy commits RAO to citizenship recognition, access to legal documentation, fair treatment, and engagement with institutions when systemic barriers affect adoptees.

02 · Community

Connection should be easier

Regional representatives, digital platforms, meetups, peer networks, and everyday conversation can make an enormous difference for people whose stories began in similar places.

03 · Heritage

Origin is not erased by adoption

RAO encourages cultural education, language resources, historical awareness, and opportunities for adoptees to explore their Russian or former-Soviet roots on their own terms.

04 · Accountability

Institutional credibility matters

The organization’s supporting-adoptees policy commits leadership to transparency, open communication, responsible resource management, and oversight as programs grow.

Leadership

RAO’s published memoranda identify David M. Schunk as President of the Russian Adoptees Organization. Other leadership, staff, volunteer, regional, and advisory positions will be published as the public roster is reviewed and kept current.

Independent organization

RAO is an adoptee organization. References to embassies, consulates, governments, or other institutions describe outreach, resources, or organizational policy and should not be read as an endorsement, government affiliation, or claim of official authority.

Built for the long term

The organization’s policy calls for annual review of its supporting-adoptees framework so it can evolve with adoptee needs and organizational growth. This website follows the same principle: publish what we know, identify what still needs work, and keep improving the infrastructure behind the community.
