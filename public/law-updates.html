Russian law, translated into what it means for adoptees.

RAO reviews official Russian legal publications and Foreign Ministry consular guidance, then explains the parts that matter to people adopted from Russia. Every substantive update on this page links back to the government source.

How to use this page

This is a change log and plain-English reference, not legal representation. “Official source” means the underlying material comes from sources such as Russia's official legal publication portal, the Russian Foreign Ministry's Consular Department, or an official Russian diplomatic mission. RAO's English explanation is our translation and interpretation for adoptee use.

Current headline for adoptees

Federal Law No. 138-FZ

Foreign adoption does not itself terminate a Russian child's citizenship.

Article 8, part 7 of the current citizenship law says a child who is a Russian citizen retains Russian citizenship when adopted by a foreign citizen or foreign citizens. The law provides a separate process through which that citizenship may be terminated. For an adult adoptee, the practical question is therefore whether a formal termination occurred in the person's individual record.

Plain-English translation: the citizenship verification process

Russian term

Проверка наличия или отсутствия гражданства Российской Федерации

Plain English: an official check to determine whether Russian government records establish that you currently have—or do not have—Russian citizenship.

When it is used

You cannot prove status with a current citizenship document

Official consular guidance describes verification when a person has no valid Russian passport or other accepted citizenship document, when such a document is expired, lost, stolen, damaged, questioned, or when other circumstances make citizenship uncertain.

What you submit

Application + questionnaire + evidence

Current consular guidance calls for the prescribed application/questionnaire and whatever documents or copies you possess that are relevant to the determination. For adoptees, Russian birth, adoption, passport, departure, and name-history records can be particularly important.

Timing

Official guidance commonly states up to two months

Current Russian consular pages cite a verification period of up to two months from registration of the application or initiation of the check, with a possible extension of up to one additional month when further checks or responses are required. Your handling office should confirm the timeline for your case.

Historical law matters

Russian citizenship questions for international adoptees cannot always be answered by reading only today's law. The current legal framework recognizes that citizenship status from earlier periods may depend on the law in effect when the relevant facts occurred. RAO therefore keeps the historical rules visible.

1991 · RSFSR Law No. 1948-I · Article 29

Russian citizenship was retained upon foreign adoption

The law provided that an RSFSR-citizen child adopted by people who were not RSFSR citizens retained RSFSR citizenship. A separate petition could terminate it if another citizenship would be provided.

2002 · Federal Law No. 62-FZ · Article 26

The same basic rule continued

The law said a Russian-citizen child adopted by a foreign citizen or foreign citizens retained Russian citizenship. Termination required a separate application and could not leave the child stateless.

2023-current · Federal Law No. 138-FZ · Article 8(7)

The rule remains explicit today

A Russian-citizen child retains citizenship upon foreign adoption. The current law provides a separate procedure for termination if statutory conditions are met and the child's interests are protected.

Current consular workflow changes adoptees should know

  • Use the official appointment system. The Foreign Ministry warns people to enter appointment systems through the official embassy or consulate website rather than search-engine links or intermediaries.
  • Appointments are free. Official Washington, New York, and Houston appointment portals say scheduling through the government system is free.
  • One applicant, one proper service slot. Some official appointment rules prohibit duplicate appointments for the same service and can delete noncompliant reservations.
  • Do citizenship verification before a new passport when status cannot be proven. Current Russian consular guidance from multiple posts instructs applicants whose citizenship first needs verification to obtain that result before scheduling the passport procedure.
  • Expect Russian-language paperwork. Citizenship verification and many supporting procedures use Russian forms. Foreign-language records can require Russian translation and, depending on the document and applicable treaty, legalization or other authentication.

Requesting records from Russia

Since the end of 2024, the Foreign Ministry and Ministry of Justice have operated under Order No. 26686/377 for requests to obtain certain personal documents from Russia. The current Foreign Ministry guidance says Russian citizens, foreign citizens, and stateless people living abroad can be applicants. The request is normally made personally through a Russian consular office; where personal appearance is impossible, the guidance says using a representative with a notarized power of attorney may be appropriate.

The same guidance specifically says identity documents, military IDs, labor books, and documents that by law require a personal application to the issuing authority cannot be obtained through this record-request channel.
