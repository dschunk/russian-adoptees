Russian citizenship & passport questions

For many adoptees, a basic question — “Am I still a Russian citizen?” — can lead into passports, adoption records, identity documents, and consular procedures. This page gives you a structured place to start.

Start with citizenship status.

RAO’s archived consular materials include requests and questionnaires used to ask Russian authorities to verify whether a person holds Russian citizenship. One bilingual request specifically frames verification as a step toward renewing an expired Russian passport.

Important distinction

The Russian Adoptees Organization is not a government agency, law firm, embassy, or consulate. We can organize information and peer resources, but only the appropriate Russian authorities can make an official determination about citizenship or issue a passport.

A practical workflow

Gather what you already have

Collect Russian birth or adoption certificates, old Russian passports, adoption records, U.S. name-change or naturalization records, and any paperwork showing your identity before and after adoption.

Build a timeline

Write down your birth name, current legal name, date and place of birth, adoption date and location in Russia, date you left Russia, and any prior Russian passport or consular information.

Identify the correct consular process

RAO’s archive contains forms for citizenship-status verification. Requirements can change, so confirm the current procedure, fees, appointment requirements, and accepted documents with the relevant Russian diplomatic or consular office before filing.

Submit complete supporting documentation

The archived questionnaires ask for copies of supporting documents and a list of what is being submitted. Keep your own complete copy of everything sent.

Use the result for your next step

If citizenship is confirmed, the result may affect what passport or consular process comes next. If it is not confirmed, ask the issuing authority what documentation or procedure applies to your specific case.

Information the archived questionnaires ask for

The bilingual adoptee-oriented questionnaire in RAO’s archive is unusually helpful because it shows the categories of information authorities may need to reconstruct an adoptee’s Russian identity history.

Documents worth locating

  • Russian birth certificate, including number, issue date, and issuing authority.
  • Russian adoption certificate or adoption-court documentation.
  • Any expired or current Russian internal or international passport.
  • Documents showing the legal name change associated with adoption.
  • Adoption agency records or translated Russian documents retained by your family.
  • Evidence of your date of departure from Russia and entry into your adoptive country.
  • Any previous correspondence with a Russian embassy or consulate.

RAO policy

Consular access is part of our mission.

RAO policy calls for developing working relationships with the Russian Embassy and consulates so adoptees have better access to resources, representation, and support when navigating passport, visa, citizenship, documentation, and other consular issues.
