RAO Adoptee Guide · Reviewed August 29, 2026

Were you adopted from Russia? You may still be a Russian citizen.

For many adoptees, adoption did not automatically end Russian citizenship. This guide explains what the law says, how to ask the Russian authorities to verify your status, what documents to gather, and what to do next if citizenship is confirmed.

The most important point

If you were already a Russian citizen when you were adopted by foreign citizens, adoption itself did not automatically terminate that citizenship under the 1991 Russian citizenship law, the 2002 citizenship law, or the current 2023 citizenship law. Citizenship could be terminated through a separate legal process. Your individual status still must be verified from the official record.

Why this matters to Russian adoptees

Russia's current citizenship law says that a Russian child adopted by foreign citizens retains Russian citizenship unless citizenship is formally terminated under the law. The same basic rule appeared in the 2002 law, and the 1991 RSFSR citizenship law likewise said an adopted Russian child retained citizenship unless the adoptive parents petitioned to end it and another citizenship would be provided.

That means an adoptee should not assume that becoming an American, Canadian, European, Australian, or other citizen automatically erased Russian citizenship. The correct question is: what does the Russian government's citizenship record show for me?

Historical cases can be complicated

The current law says citizenship status from an earlier period is determined under the law that was in force when the relevant events happened. People adopted during the Soviet period, people born outside the Russian SFSR, and people with unusual family or citizenship histories may require a more individualized review.

The step-by-step process

1. Gather every Russian document you have

Start with your Russian birth certificate, adoption certificate, Russian court decree, old Russian international passport, internal passport if one ever existed, orphanage paperwork, adoption-agency translations, and any prior correspondence with a Russian embassy or consulate. Do not discard expired passports.

2. Build your identity timeline

Write down your Russian birth name, every later legal name, date and place of birth, last address or registration in Russia if known, orphanage or institution, adoption date, city or region of adoption, date you left Russia, and any known passport numbers or issuing authorities. This is exactly the kind of history the citizenship-verification questionnaires ask authorities to reconstruct.

3. Find the Russian consular office responsible for where you live

Outside Russia, citizenship matters are handled through Russian diplomatic missions and consular offices. Use the Russian Foreign Ministry's official consular directory. Do not rely on a random third-party appointment site.

4. Ask for verification of the presence or absence of Russian citizenship

The official Russian term is проверка наличия или отсутствия гражданства Российской Федерации — verification of whether Russian citizenship is present or absent. The procedure is governed by Federal Law No. 138-FZ, Presidential Decree No. 889, and the joint МВД/МИД verification order No. 753/20664.

5. Use the current verification form

The verification forms were amended by МВД/МИД Order No. 1033/26702, dated December 8, 2025 and registered January 19, 2026. Older forms circulating in adoption groups may therefore be outdated. Always download or confirm the current form from the consulate handling your case before filing.

6. Prepare supporting evidence

Expect to provide identity documents and copies of Russian records you possess. The adoptee-oriented questionnaires ask about Russian and current names, birth information, last Russian address, date of departure, prior Russian passport details, adoption date and location, birth and adoption certificates, known biological-parent information, and a list of supporting documents. Some foreign-issued documents may require legalization and Russian translation; confirm the exact requirements with your consular office.

7. Book the appointment from the official consulate website

The Russian Foreign Ministry advises applicants to enter the appointment system through the official embassy or consulate website rather than through search results or paid intermediaries. Where electronic scheduling is used, choose the correct consular service, register, confirm your email, and reserve an available appointment. If no slot is available, the official system may place you on a waiting list.

8. Attend and submit the complete packet

Citizenship applications are generally submitted personally in the required written form. Bring originals plus the copies requested by your office, your photographs if required, the completed current questionnaire, and proof of any required consular fees. Keep a complete duplicate of everything you submit.

9. Wait for the official determination

The consular office may need to send inquiries to Russian authorities to reconstruct old records. Adoption-era files can span civil-registry offices, passport authorities, courts, orphanages, and regional agencies. A delay does not by itself mean citizenship is absent.

10. If citizenship is confirmed, move to the passport process

Once Russian citizenship is officially confirmed, ask the consular office what proof will be accepted for a passport application. Russia's current passport guidance requires a passport application and proof of Russian identity/citizenship; expired Russian travel documents can be important evidence. Passport processing is a separate procedure from citizenship verification.

What the current law says about adoption

1991 law · Article 29

Adoption did not automatically remove citizenship

The RSFSR law stated that a child who was an RSFSR citizen retained that citizenship when adopted by people who were not RSFSR citizens. Termination required a separate petition and another citizenship for the child.

2002 law · Article 26

The rule continued

The 2002 federal citizenship law stated that a Russian child adopted by foreign citizens retained Russian citizenship, with termination handled separately on application.

Current law · 138-FZ, Article 8(7)

The principle remains in force

The current law again provides that a Russian child adopted by foreign citizens retains Russian citizenship unless citizenship is terminated through the legally prescribed process.

If you are missing Russian records

The Russian Foreign Ministry maintains a formal process for requesting certain personal documents from Russia. Russian citizens, foreign citizens, and stateless persons living outside Russia may use the process. The current guidance says the request is normally submitted personally to a Russian consular office; if personal appearance is impossible, a representative acting under a notarized power of attorney may be appropriate. Foreign documents generally must be legalized when required and translated into Russian.

This process does not allow you to order identity documents such as passports, and not every adoption or court record is necessarily obtainable through this route. Ask the consular office which agency holds the specific record you need.

After citizenship is confirmed: applying for a Russian passport

The Foreign Ministry's current passport instructions provide separate applications for biometric and non-biometric passports. For applicants age 14 and older, the non-biometric application is submitted in two copies; biometric applications use a different form. The current guidance also states that foreign-language documents that are not duplicated in Russian must be translated into Russian, with the translation or translator's signature notarized in the prescribed manner.

Why RAO exists

No adoptee should have to decode this alone.

Government websites are written for legal procedure, not for someone trying to reconstruct a life split across countries, names, records, and languages. RAO's job is to translate the process into plain English, preserve the official-source links, collect adoptee experience, and help people arrive at the consulate prepared.
